Customer briefing

Find real application risk before your customers do

Strix is a self-hosted AI pentesting assistant. It tests authorized code, APIs, and live apps, then returns findings with proof your team can act on.

11 slides · ~14 minutes

The gap

You ship faster than a traditional pentest can keep up

What teams feel

Features move continuously. Security reviews arrive late, or only on the systems someone remembered to book. High-severity issues still show up in production and in customer questionnaires.

What scanners miss

SAST, DAST, and dependency scans are necessary. They do not reliably prove authorization flaws, business-logic abuse, or AI/MCP failure modes — and they rarely give a developer a path they can reproduce.

What Strix is

An AI pentest assistant. Not a rubber stamp.

What it does

Attacks applications you authorize — source, URLs, APIs, and related surfaces — and returns severity, impact, reproduction steps, and a proof of concept when it can demonstrate the issue.

What it does not do

It does not close residual risk for you, replace a scoped assessment when a customer or auditor requires one, or test anything you do not own or have written permission to test.

Salient features

What you can count on

Attacks and proves

Agents exploit authorized apps and attach a PoC and reproduction steps when they can validate the issue — not a lint list.

Real pentest toolkit

HTTP proxy, browser, shell, exploit runtime, recon, and SAST/DAST — the same class of tools a human tester uses.

Multi-agent graph

Specialized agents split recon, exploitation, and follow-on work, share discoveries, and chain issues like a small red team.

CLI and dashboard

One engine: headless CLI for CI, or a self-hosted dashboard for orgs, schedules, PR reviews, and a findings queue.

Artifact-driven

Coverage maps and scan outputs are durable files — reports, SARIF, findings — not a chat transcript that vanishes.

Matched to standards

Pin a pentest to OWASP Top 10, ASVS, NIST, PCI DSS, and related maps. Findings are tagged to the standard and CWE.

Evidence you can act on

Severity, impact, remediation guidance, markdown, JSON, and SARIF so results land in the tools you already use.

Fits how you ship

PR comments, CI gates, scheduled staging scans, and agent skills so review starts before merge.

Honest coverage

Each run shows what was tested and skipped. A green dashboard is not a pass. People still own residual risk.

Where it fits

One motion across the work you already do

Application security

White-box review of a repository plus live-target testing, so engineers get a finding they can reproduce — not a CVE ID with no path.

Rapid pentest

Hours, not weeks, on a staging URL or service you are authorized to hit — before a launch, a customer demo, or a high-risk change.

CI and pull requests

Scan the diff, comment on the PR, and fail the job when real issues appear. Review starts before merge, not after release.

AI and modern APIs

The same engine covers web, REST/GraphQL, and LLM/MCP surfaces when those products are in scope — without a second toolchain.

How it works

Artifact-driven. Your environment. Your model.

  • Artifact-driven architecture — coverage maps and scan outputs are durable files (standards markdown, reports, SARIF, findings), not a vanishing chat transcript.
  • CLI or self-hosted dashboard — same engine for a named target, scheduled scans, PR reviews, and a findings queue.
  • Isolated execution — each scan runs in its own sandbox so attack tooling does not sit on an engineer’s laptop.
  • You choose the LLM — bring your provider and keys; you are not locked to a vendor pentest cloud.

What you get

Evidence, artifacts, and a standard you can name

Findings with proof

Severity, impact, reproduction steps, and a PoC where the assistant could demonstrate the issue — tagged to the standard and CWE.

Durable artifacts

Markdown reports, JSON, and SARIF so results land in GitHub, GitLab, and existing issue workflows — and survive after the scan ends.

Mapped to standards

Match a pentest to OWASP Top 10, ASVS, NIST, PCI DSS, and related maps. Coverage shows which rows were tested. Silence is not a pass.

How you stay in control

Your people own the decision. Strix does the grunt work.

  • Only authorized testers start scans, and only against assets you own or have written permission to test.
  • Findings are proposed evidence. Your team confirms, disputes, or defers before residual-risk language changes.
  • Live targets require domain verification. An unverified host is not treated as in-scope.
  • Secrets stay out of tickets. Incomplete or demo modes are labeled — never presented as a finished pentest.

Clear limits

A strong assistant. Not a complete pentest on every run.

Coverage has a budget

Scan mode, time, and target type cap what gets exercised. A clean dashboard means nothing was proven in what was tested — not that the product is safe.

Humans still sign off

Scoped external assessments, customer questionnaires, and residual-risk acceptance stay with your security program. Strix makes that program faster and better evidenced.

How to start

A time-boxed pilot on the apps you actually ship

  • Pick 2–3 production-adjacent apps — staging URLs and repositories you are authorized to test.
  • Name an AppSec owner and a small authorized-tester group. Residual-risk wording stays with them.
  • Turn on PR scanning for those repos and a scheduled scan on staging.
  • Review at the end of the pilot: validated findings, time to a working PoC, and whether coverage is trusted enough to keep.

Discussion

What should we put in scope first?

The useful next conversation is which applications you are authorized to test, who may start scans, and what ‘done’ means for residual risk at the end of a pilot.
